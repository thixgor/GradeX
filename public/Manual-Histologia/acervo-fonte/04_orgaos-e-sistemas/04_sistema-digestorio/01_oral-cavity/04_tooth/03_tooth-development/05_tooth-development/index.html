Cap stage of tooth development (early)

The cap stage results from a differential rate of cell proliferation in the epithelial cells of the bud, resulting in a concave structure resembling a cup. During this stage, histodifferentiation occurs establishing the enamel organ which encloses ectomesenchymal cells that develop into the dental papilla. The dental papilla forms the dentin and pulp. 400x

Tooth germ

The tooth germ is first identifiable at the cap stage of tooth development (histodifferentiation). The tooth germ consists of all the identifiable ectoderm and ectomesenchyme tissues that form the tooth: inner and outer enamel epithelia, (ectoderm), dental papillae and the dental follicle (ectomesenchyme).
