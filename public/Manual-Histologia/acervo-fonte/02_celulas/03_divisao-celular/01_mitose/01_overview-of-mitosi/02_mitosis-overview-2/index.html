Mitosis

Mitosis is the process whereby cells divide to produce two identical daughter cells. During the mitosis phase of the cell cycle, chromosomal segregation (karyokinesis) and cell division (cytokinesis) occur. The stages of mitosis are prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase. The remainder of the cell cycle is called interphase.

Interphase

Interphase is the longest phase of the cell cycle. The nucleus is distinct, surrounded by an obvious nuclear envelope; individual chromosomes are not visible as distinct structures. During interphase in proliferating cells, chromosome replication and diplosome duplication occur.

Prophase

During prophase, chromosomes condense, diplosomes move to opposite poles of the nucleus, the mitotic spindle assembles and the nuclear envelope and the nucleolus disappear.

Anaphase

Anaphase begins abruptly as sister chromatids separate and are drawn toward the opposite poles of the cell. The end of anaphase is marked by the segregation of an identical set of chromosomes at each pole.
