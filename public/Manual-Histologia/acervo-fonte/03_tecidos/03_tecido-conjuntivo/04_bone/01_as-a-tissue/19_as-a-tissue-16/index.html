Bone classification

Bone tissue is classified based on two features: (1) its macroscopic appearance, i.e., how it appears to the naked eye, as either compact (cortical) or spongy (cancellous) and (2) its stage of maturation as either woven (immature) or lamellar (mature). A complete classification of bone tissue includes one term from each set, e.g., compact, woven bone or spongy, lamellar bone. 400x (all)

Compact bone

Compact bone appears as a solid mass to the naked eye. Histologically, the volume of bone exceeds the volume of non-bone tissue within any given area.

Woven bone

The matrix of woven bone is not organized into lamellae, osteocyte lacunae are numerous and rounded, and the matrix often stains faintly bluish. All bone is initially deposited as woven bone.

Lamellar bone

The matrix of lamellar bone is organized into lamellae that can be arranged as concentric layers forming osteons; flattened plates on the internal and external surfaces of a bone; or as parallel plates forming spicules of spongy bone. Woven compact and spongy bone are gradually remodeled into lamellar bone.
